How to write a card (this is the part readers actually see, so it decides whether they keep scrolling):

- `emoji`: one emoji that fits the story's vibe (not just its topic).
- `punchy`: the headline rewritten so a scroller stops, ≤ 12 words. It must still say what happened.
  - **Never** just shorten or reword the original headline. Find the angle: the surprise, the stakes,
    the irony, the "wait, what?", the number that matters, or who wins and who loses.
  - **Never** use the newswire shape "Company raises $X to do Y" or "X says Y". Never start with the
    company name followed by a verb like raises, bags, nets, secures, announces, launches.
  - Keep the famous name if there is one (Arsenal, Apple, Nvidia, Modi, RBI): it's what makes people stop.
    Lead with the twist, not the setup.
  - Funding rounds: say what the money is betting on. Policy: say who it hits. Markets: say what moved and why
    anyone should care.
  - Good (original → punchy):
    - "Simplismart raises $14M Series B led by Accel, NVIDIA participates" → "Nvidia wants AI to be cheaper to run. It just paid $14M for that."
    - "RBI keeps repo rate unchanged at 5.5%" → "RBI hits snooze on interest rates. Again."
    - "Instagram now lets users upload 90-minute videos" → "Instagram is coming for YouTube with 90-minute uploads"
    - "Jio Platforms lists at 21% premium, valued at Rs 12.5 lakh crore" → "Jio's stock debut just minted a ₹12.5 lakh crore giant"
    - "Spider sleep study finds REM-like rebound" → "Spiders might dream. Scientists caught them catching up on sleep."
    - "Sudan famine risk rises as civil war grinds on" → "Sudan's war is pushing millions towards famine"
    - "Fleetwood 1-1 Arsenal (5-3 pens): champions dumped out of Carabao Cup" → "League Two Fleetwood just knocked out champions Arsenal"
  - Bad: "Live2.ai raises ₹130 Cr to scale AI marketing SaaS" (that is just the headline), "Apple refreshes
    Macs with M6 chip" (so does every press release), "Fleetwood beat champions on penalties" (lost the famous name), "Big news in AI
    funding!" (says nothing), "You won't believe what RBI did" (clickbait).
- `so_what`: one line, ≤ 22 words, talking to the reader: why this matters to them or to the bigger
  pattern (`evidence_of`, `links_to_last_week`). **Never** start with "Evidence", "Shows", "Signals",
  "This", "Indicates" or "Highlights". No analyst-speak. Good: "Money is chasing the boring plumbing of AI now,
  not shiny chatbots." Bad: "Evidence that AI infrastructure startups are attracting strategic capital."
- `highlight`: the 2–6 most important words copied **exactly** from `so_what` (same spelling), usually the
  number, the name or the twist. Empty string if nothing deserves it.
