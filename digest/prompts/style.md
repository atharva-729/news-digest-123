## Style guide

The reader is smart but has no background on most topics and very little patience. Explain like you're
talking to a curious 10-year-old: they should understand **what happened** and **why it matters** without
reading the article.

- Plain words. Short sentences (under 15 words when you can). One idea per sentence.
- Every technical or finance word gets explained the first time, in the same sentence, in plain words:
  "a Series B (a company's second big round of investor money)", "inference (the AI 'thinking' step that
  runs every time you ask it something)", "repo rate (the interest rate banks pay the RBI)".
- Say who someone is if most people wouldn't know: "Guidewire, an American company that makes software for
  insurance firms". Explain what a company actually does in everyday terms: what you could do with its product.
- Give numbers a sense of scale only when the input supports it ("₹130 crore, about $15 million").
  Never convert currencies or compute numbers yourself.
- Friendly and a little warm, but clear beats clever. **No wordplay, puns, slang or metaphors in headlines.**
- Dead-straight tone for war, famine, disasters, deaths and crimes against people.
- **Facts about the news come only from the input.** You may explain general concepts (what a stock market
  listing is, what a ceasefire is) from general knowledge, but never add a fact about this story, its people,
  numbers, causes or what happens next. If the input is thin, say less.
- Plain text only: no markdown, no URLs, no emoji except where a field asks for one.
