🤖 Simplismart raises $14 million for AI inference infrastructure

Simplismart raised $14 million in a Series B led by Dallas Venture Capital, with NVIDIA, Accel, Shastra VC and Micromax participating. The Bengaluru startup builds software that helps enterprises deploy and run AI models, and helps cloud providers sell GPU capacity as managed AI services. It says its annualised revenue run rate grew 14x in six months.

Why it matters: More capital should help it expand beyond India as demand rises for cheaper, faster AI inference.

📣 Live2.ai raises Rs 130 crore for AI marketing software

Live2.ai raised Rs 130 crore in a pre-Series B round led by IvyCap Ventures, with a secondary component for early shareholders. The Bengaluru startup sells AI software for social intelligence and commerce activation: it tracks brand conversations, manages social publishing and compliance, and turns content into shoppable posts. It says it works with more than 200 brands and gets 90% of revenue from outside India.

Why it matters: The funding should help it expand its sales to enterprise and global consumer brands.

🏥 Cent raises $15 million and buys AI radiology platform Medsee

Cent, the preventive healthcare startup founded by Practo co-founder Shashank ND, raised $15 million in a Series A led by Blume Ventures and South Park Commons. It also acquired Medsee, an AI radiology platform. Cent uses imaging, biomarkers and AI for early disease detection, and says about 10,000 people have been screened through its protocol.

Why it matters: Cent can expand its screening business and AI tools as it tries to detect disease earlier.

🐟 Scientists count millions of eels at a deep-sea mountain off Tasmania

Researchers studying Patience Seamount south of Tasmania say basketwork eels gather there in huge numbers, likely to reproduce. A team led by CSIRO marine scientist Ben Scoulding estimates the peak aggregation could reach 2.8 million eels, weighing more than 3,500 metric tons.

Why it matters: The survey puts a rare deep-sea fish gathering at a scale scientists had not measured before.

No pattern yet Original: ScienceAlert ↗

🚀 Mystery metal object in Minnesota may be a rocket part

A shiny, bowl-shaped object about 11 feet wide was found in a Minnesota cornfield and taken to a secure location. Federal officials are investigating, and experts quoted in the story think it may be debris from a Chinese LandSpace Zhuque-2E rocket.

Why it matters: Authorities are still trying to confirm the object’s origin and whether more debris exists.
