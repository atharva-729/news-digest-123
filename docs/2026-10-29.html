🛡️ Google says Gemini 4 Carbon tops its cybersecurity and coding benchmarks

💊 India cuts prices on 543 anti-cancer drug brands

The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority has revised maximum retail prices for 543 brands of anti-cancer medicines, with new rates effective November 9. Some cuts are steep: Dr Reddy’s Azacytin drops from ₹6,337 to ₹1,495.04, and RPG Life Sciences’ Anarzole falls from ₹695.76 to ₹73 for 10 tablets. The list covers different strengths and pack sizes.

Why it matters: Cancer treatment should become cheaper for patients, while drug makers face capped trade margins on 137 non-scheduled anti-cancer medicines.

The agency says land, public funds and contractor money were diverted to the university.

Why it matters: The ED’s attachment covers land and buildings worth Rs 426.46 crore.

Why it matters: Forensic tests will determine whether the liquor was contaminated and linked to the deaths.

🌍 World

🗳️ Netanyahu bloc leads in Israel vote but falls short of majority

With 85.6% of ballot boxes counted, partial results showed the pro-Netanyahu bloc on 53 seats and Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc on 48 in the 120-seat Knesset. Arab-led parties were on 19 seats, and turnout reached 77.7%, the highest in more than two decades. Exit polls and official counting still leave neither bloc near the 61 seats needed to govern.

Why it matters: Coalition talks could last for weeks because no bloc has a clear path to 61 seats.

Original: Indian Express World ↗
🌍 World

🗳️ Israel’s special ballots could still change the final seat count

Israel still has hundreds of thousands of double-envelope votes to count after regular ballots left Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc short of a majority. These are special ballots for soldiers, reservists, displaced residents and others who could not vote at their home polling station. The Central Elections Committee must verify voters did not vote twice before adding them to the tally.

Why it matters: Those votes may move the final Knesset arithmetic and affect who gets the first shot at building a government.

🌎 Flávio Bolsonaro arrives in the US after losing Brazil election

Brazilian senator Flávio Bolsonaro arrived in Miami with his family after refusing to concede defeat to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Brazil’s Supreme Court had ordered him to surrender his passports within 24 hours, citing risk of flight in a money-laundering probe. The US State Department said it would grant visas, and Donald Trump said he approved of their entry.

Why it matters: The move could deepen tensions between Brazil and the United States as the legal fight around Bolsonaro continues.

📈 Fed holds rates steady as inflation stays sticky

The article says stubborn inflation, higher energy costs, tariffs and AI-driven demand are keeping price pressures elevated.

Why it matters: Traders now look to the next inflation data for clues on whether the Fed resumes tightening.

📈 Markets

🛢️ World Bank says war could push commodity prices up 23%

The World Bank expects commodity prices to rise 23% in 2026 if Middle East conflict worsens and Russian oil infrastructure keeps taking damage. It projects energy prices to jump 32%, oil supply to fall 5.2% this year, and Brent to average $92 a barrel. Food and metals are also expected to rise, with metals up 25% and precious metals up 40%.

Why it matters: Higher energy and metals costs could feed inflation, food insecurity and slower growth worldwide.
