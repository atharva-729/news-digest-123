📦 Zorit raises Rs 27 crore for its retailer-focused quick commerce model

Zorit, formerly Bijnis, raised nearly Rs 27 crore in a round led by Sparrow Capital, with Info Edge, WaterBridge, Better Capital, angels and employees also joining. The Bengaluru-based startup helps offline retailers source stock from locally held dark stores, meaning nearby warehouses, with delivery in as little as 60 minutes. It says it now runs 12 dark stores in 12 cities and has annualised sales of Rs 120 crore.

Why it matters: Fresh capital will help Zorit expand its quick commerce infrastructure and product range for offline retailers.

💊 India cuts prices on 543 anti-cancer medicine brands

The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority has revised maximum retail prices for 543 brands of anti-cancer medicines, with new rates from November 9. Some cuts are steep: RPG Life Sciences’ Anarzole drops from ₹695.76 to ₹73 for 10 tablets, and Dr Reddy’s Azacytin falls from ₹6,337 to ₹1,495.04. The list covers different strengths and pack sizes and will be updated as manufacturers file more data.

Why it matters: Drugmakers must sell these brands within the new price caps from November 9.

🍶 Police say AI searches helped build a spurious liquor racket in Dewas

Police in Madhya Pradesh’s Dewas district say 26-year-old Ajay Rathore used AI tools to look up how to make liquor at home, then used the internet to find suppliers and buy chemicals from Indore. Investigators say the liquor was poured into discarded bottles, mixed with genuine stock, and sold in rural areas. They are checking whether it was linked to 13 suspected hooch deaths in Chandana and Binjana villages.

Why it matters: Forensic tests and the broader probe will show whether the liquor caused the suspected deaths and who else was involved.

🌍 World

🗳️ Israel’s special ballots could still change the seat count

Israel’s double-envelope votes are special ballots cast by eligible voters who cannot vote at their regular polling stations, such as soldiers, reservists and hospital patients. Around 600,000 to 650,000 or more of these ballots were still waiting to be counted, according to reports cited in the piece. The Central Elections Committee must verify that each voter did not already vote elsewhere before adding them to the tally.

Why it matters: Those ballots could shift the final seat split and affect which leader gets the first chance to form a government.

📈 Markets

🛢️ World Bank sees commodity prices jump on Middle East war

The World Bank says global commodity prices could surge 23% in 2026, with energy prices projected to rise 32% and Brent averaging $92 a barrel. It warns that more conflict in the Middle East, plus damage to Russian oil infrastructure, could push prices even higher. The report also sees food prices rising more than 10% this year and metals rising 25% in 2026.

Why it matters: Higher commodity prices could keep inflation elevated and squeeze growth across importing economies.
