🦖 Rare Nanotyrannus skeleton sells for $47.3 million at Sotheby’s

Sotheby’s sold the most complete Nanotyrannus skeleton, nicknamed Jodi, for $47.3 million after a 12-minute bidding battle. The roughly 67-million-year-old fossil was found in Montana in 2019 and is one of only three mostly complete Nanotyrannus skeletons known. Scientists have only recently argued that Nanotyrannus is its own genus, separate from T. rex.

Why it matters: The sale pushes dinosaur fossils deeper into the high-end collector market and out of reach for many museums.

🎈 Fun

🌊 Researchers identify first known fully marine amphibian

Scientists say two near-complete fossils from southwestern China show a new species, Gerrothorax maritimus, that lived entirely in the sea about 237 million years ago. The armored amphibian had a flat body and features that may have kept salt water from dehydrating it. The fossils were found in deep-sea sediments in Guizhou Province alongside an ancient marine reptile.

Why it matters: The find changes what scientists thought amphibians could survive and may help conservation research on salt tolerance.
